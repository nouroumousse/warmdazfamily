## A language is made of everyday moments

A language does not live only in dictionaries or archives. It lives in greetings at the doorway, in the names we give to places, in jokes shared around a table and in the stories we hear more than once.

Tachelhit carries ways of seeing the world that are deeply connected to the communities who speak it. Every expression holds a little history; every conversation gives that history another day of life.

## Make space for the words

Keeping a language present does not need to begin with a large project. It can begin at home, with a question asked to an elder, a voice note saved with permission, or a word written down before it is forgotten.

- Ask relatives about words and expressions you do not know.
- Record stories only with the speaker's permission.
- Share what you learn with younger family members.
- Welcome different pronunciations and local ways of speaking.

## The next generation is part of the story

Young people should not have to choose between belonging to their heritage and participating in the wider world. They can do both. Digital tools, photography, audio and writing can help make local knowledge visible, as long as the people and context behind that knowledge remain respected.

A living language is not a finished project. It is a relationship — renewed every time we use it.
